# Universal evaluation dimensions

These 11 dimensions are checked on **every product**, regardless of domain. Domain packs add specific specs/standards on top; they do not replace this list.

## 1. Functional fit vs. requirements
Does the candidate satisfy the user's must-haves? How many nice-to-haves does it hit? Score 0–5 against the weighted requirements doc from step 4.

## 2. Standards compliance & compatibility
- **Regulatory / safety**: CE (EU), FCC (US), UL (US), RoHS (EU restriction of hazardous substances), CCC (China), Energy Star (US), EPREL (EU energy label), TÜV/GS marks.
- **Domain interop**: the standards from `domain-<name>.md` (bike: BB shell, axle, brake mount; PC: socket, RAM gen, PCIe; etc.).
- **Ecosystem compatibility**: cross-check candidates against the user's existing gear from step 3 context.

## 3. Authorized sourcing & counterfeit risk
- **Authorized dealer status** — verify against manufacturer's authorized-dealer list (often searchable on the brand's website).
- **Serial / hologram / GS1 QR** — verifiable on manufacturer site for many premium brands.
- **Return policy** — window, restocking fee, shipping cost. Buyers who skip this overpay when something doesn't fit.
- **Counterfeit risk** — high-margin small items (cables, batteries, chips, optics) are highest risk. Marketplace third-party sellers without strong reputation = red flag.

## 4. Quality & reliability signals
- Independent reviews from reputable sources (RTINGS, Wirecutter, DPReview, GamersNexus, Project Farm).
- Benchmark / MTBF / durability data where available.
- User-reported failure modes (forums, subreddits, Amazon Q&A — read the 3-star reviews especially).
- Long-term-ownership reviews (r/BuyItForLife, "2 years later" YouTube).

## 5. Repairability
- iFixit repairability score where available.
- EU repairability index (A–E scale, mandatory since June 2025 for smartphones/tablets).
- Parts availability — does the manufacturer sell replacement parts? Third parties?
- Right-to-repair stance — proprietary connectors, soldered components, anti-repair design.

## 6. Total cost of ownership (TCO) — landed-cost over horizon
Components, all in user's currency for the user's region, over the stated horizon (default 3 yrs):
- Purchase (at target price, not MSRP)
- Accessories required to use the product
- Consumables (ink, filters, chains, beans, batteries)
- Energy (operating power × hours × kWh price)
- Maintenance / service
- Expected repairs (warranty period vs. horizon)
- Insurance / extended warranty NET of credit-card-provided coverage
- Financing cost (0% taksit, BNPL interest if any)
- − Rebates (manufacturer, retailer, CC sign-up bonuses)
- − Resale value at horizon end (use eBay sold-median for the SKU at age N)
- + Opportunity cost note for strategic purchases (what else could the money do)

## 7. Vendor / supplier risk
- Brand reliability data — recall history, owner-satisfaction surveys.
- Warranty terms + regional coverage — verify the brand's authorized distributor in the user's region honors warranty for the specific SKU; international / global warranty exists for some brands but is region-locked for many.
- Parts availability — official vs. third-party, lead time.
- Service network — authorized service centers in user's region.
- EOL signals — has the model been refreshed? Are stocks being cleared?
- Supply-chain stability — recent shortages, geopolitical risk, single-manufacturer dependence.
- Single-source-of-failure / ecosystem lock-in — proprietary battery, walled-garden ecosystem, niche connector.
- IoT cybersecurity & privacy practices — vendor's data handling, firmware update cadence, IoT cert marks.

## 8. Upgrade path & lock-in
- Can the product be improved later (modular vs. monolithic)?
- Is it standards-based or proprietary?
- What does switching to a different ecosystem cost?

## 9. Sourcing leverage
- Price spread across retailers (output from step 6).
- Viable substitutes that preserve must-haves at lower cost.
- Known discount windows on the calendar (next BF / Prime Day / model-refresh).
- Bundle opportunities (often the only seller-flex point).

## 10. Decision risk / regret factors
Output of the pre-mortem in step 8.5: top-3 future-regret scenarios + mitigations.

## 11. ESG / sustainability (optional weighting)
Only included when user signals interest:
- Carbon footprint disclosure (manufacturer ESG reports).
- Conflict materials (cobalt, tantalum, gold sourcing).
- Recyclability + end-of-life path.
- Repairability (overlaps with dimension 5).
- Brand's labor practices / fair-trade certifications where applicable.
