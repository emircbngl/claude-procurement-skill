# Sourcing & negotiation playbook

Once the fair-price band is known (from step 6) and the candidate is qualified (steps 5, 7), this module turns those into the **buying tactic**: where to buy, what levers to pull, when to wait.

## Best channel selection

For the recommended candidate, surface the **single best channel** based on:

- Price closest to target / aspirational band
- Authorized dealer status (✓ required for warranty)
- Strong return policy (≥14 days TR, ≥30 days preferred)
- Fast / free shipping in user's region
- Stock available (no 6-week backorder unless user is fine with it)
- No red flags from step 6.7

Output: "Buy from <retailer> at <price>, channel link: <url>".

## Negotiation tactics

Not every channel is negotiable. Treat as a hierarchy:

| Channel | Negotiation room |
|---|---|
| Online retailer first-party (Amazon / Best Buy / Newegg / John Lewis / equivalent regional first-party) | Low — price-match policies are the lever |
| Marketplace third-party | Sometimes — message seller with competitor quote |
| Local brick-and-mortar | High — especially end of month/quarter |
| Specialist dealer (bike shop, camera shop) | High — particularly on bundled accessories/service |
| Direct from manufacturer | Low — promo windows only |

### The eight levers

1. **Price match** — most large retailers will match a documented authorized-dealer price. Ask in writing.
2. **Bulk / multi-unit** — even 2-unit asks unlock B2B-leaning discounts.
3. **Cash vs financed** — Cash discount (2–5%) common in many markets where cash reduces seller's payment-processing cost. Conversely, 0% installment plans / BNPL (e.g., taksit in Turkey, Klarna / Affirm / Afterpay in Western markets, Tabby / Tamara in MENA) shift cost to seller margin and can hide a discount opportunity.
4. **Package / bundle deals** — sellers protect headline SKU price but flex on accessories, extended warranty, or service.
5. **Walk-away with alternatives** — explicit reference to a peer SKU at lower price is the single strongest lever.
6. **Loyalty desk / manager escalation** — different price authority.
7. **End-of-month / end-of-quarter** — commission-driven channels (cars, furniture, high-end electronics).
8. **Anchor with competitor written quote** — exploit anchoring effect; bring the printed quote.

### Advanced tactics

- **Decoy framing** — request quotes on 3 similar SKUs simultaneously. Middle option becomes the "reasonable" choice.
- **Dealer-discount FIRST, then rebate** — negotiate the dealer's discount before mentioning a manufacturer rebate. Order matters; mentioning rebate first lets the dealer hold price.
- **Credit-card sign-up bonus stack** — if user is opening a new card anyway, time the purchase to satisfy the spend requirement. Net cost reduction.
- **Manufacturer rebate submission** — track separately. Rebates are often forgotten and unclaimed; calendar reminder is part of the lifecycle plan.
- **Loss-leader bundles at major retail** — wait for known seasonal windows (next section).

## Discount calendar

Surface to the user when the next likely discount window is:

| Window | Typical depth | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Black Friday / Cyber Monday (late Nov, global) | ~25–35% off electronics on average | Most consumer electronics |
| Prime Day (mid-June + mid-Oct, Amazon regions) | Matches BF on Amazon SKUs | Anything Amazon stocks |
| Model-refresh dumps | 20–40% on outgoing model | Cameras, GPUs, phones — follows CES (Jan), MWC (Feb/Mar), WWDC (Jun), IFA (Sep) |
| End of fiscal quarter (calendar Q1/Q2/Q3/Q4 or vendor-specific) | Variable, rep-driven | B2B / specialty / cars |
| Boxing Day / post-holiday clearance (late Dec) | 10–25% | Toys, holiday inventory |
| Back-to-school (Aug, NH region) | 10–20% | Laptops, peripherals, dorm goods |
| 11.11 / Singles Day (Nov, originally CN, now global) | 15–35% | Asian-marketplace SKUs and global crossovers |
| Regional seasonal events (varies by country) | 10–30% | Local equivalents — e.g., Diwali (India, Oct/Nov), Spring sale events, national-day promotions |

If the user's timeline is flexible and a known window is < 60 days out, **recommend waiting**. If timeline is tight, recommend buying now.

## Substitutes that preserve must-haves

If the primary candidate is over walk-away price and discount window is far off, surface 1–2 substitutes that preserve must-haves at a lower price tier. These come from step 6's peer-tier benchmark.

## Output to report

The "Negotiation playbook" section:
- **Best channel + price** (specific URL + price + why)
- **Levers worth pulling** at this channel
- **Calendar** — next 2 likely discount windows + estimated depth
- **Substitutes** if waiting isn't acceptable + their fair-price targets
